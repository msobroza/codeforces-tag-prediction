Each exercise comes with its statement and one accepted Python solution. The task statement asks for the eight focus tags; an exercise can have several, or none (46% have none). Every tag counts equally in the macro F1, so the rare tags such as probabilities and games weigh as much as math. Goals (PROBLEM.md): per-tag metrics, a command line that predicts and evaluates, and a prediction in under ten seconds. My constraint: everything trains and runs on one consumer GPU, an RTX 2080 Ti with 11 GB of memory. It shaped the methods: ModernBERT is tuned on one fold and the large model gets LoRA, F2LLM's 0.6-billion-parameter encoder stays frozen, and on the GPU every prediction goes one problem at a time. The data and the paper's baselines come from xCodeEval, by Khan and colleagues, published at ACL 2024 (arXiv 2303.03004).
