[2] K. Sechidis, G. Tsoumakas, I. Vlahavas. On the Stratification of Multi-Label Data. ECML PKDD 2011.

The first step finds problems that are versions of each other: TF-IDF on the descriptions proposes 203 pairs at cosine 0.4 or more, and two independent verdicts from Claude Opus 5.5 keep the 82 that are easy and hard versions of one problem; the verdicts agree on 484 of the 485 pairs they judged. Then iterative stratification, the method of Sechidis, Tsoumakas and Vlahavas from ECML PKDD 2011, places whole groups: it takes the rarest label first and puts each of its groups in the fold that lacks it most, balancing the 8 tags, the 9 pairs shared by 20 or more problems and the release year; problems without a focus tag are stratified apart, by year. The test fifth, 994 problems, is scored once, and mirrors the data: math is 28.3% of it as of all problems, and the worst tag, number theory, is 7.2% against 7.0%; the dev part is cut again into five folds the same way. Against 50 random splits, the frozen one leaks no pair (random by problem leaks 26 on average) and its worst tag is 3.1% off its share, against 28% for random group splits. The split is frozen in assets/split/split.csv.
