Each dot is a method's F1 on the 994 test problems, each line its 95% interval from a group bootstrap: 2,000 resamples of the near-duplicate groups, so a pair of versions moves together. The top row is the macro F1, the mean of the eight tags; the number after each tag is how many test problems carry it. Intervals widen, broadly, as tags get rarer: plus or minus 0.04 to 0.05 for math with 281 problems, 0.13 to 0.15 for probabilities with 18; games stays narrower than geometry because its F1 is close to 1. That is why a few points of F1 on a rare tag are noise. Where the methods truly differ: on graphs and number theory, the three LLM-based methods beat TF-IDF by 0.11 to 0.18, and every paired difference excludes zero. On strings, trees and geometry every interval overlaps. On macro F1, the three LLM-based methods lie within about 0.01 of each other, all clearly above TF-IDF. These are test scores, reported once; the choice was made on dev.
