Up and left is better: higher test macro F1, faster prediction. Two LLM-based families lead: TypeSafe Jev (0.737 and 0.751) answers in a quarter of a second; the dense-embedding methods (F2LLM, 0.730 to 0.748) answer in 0.13 to 0.14 seconds on the GPU, for free, against three to four seconds on a CPU, though their dev scores (0.70 to 0.71) are below Jev's (0.73). LLM-based DesCode2Tag, OpenAI gpt-6-luna reading the whole problem, reaches 0.741 in about a second. The fine-tuned cross-encoder classifiers (ModernBERT) and TF-IDF sit near 0.69 to 0.70; LLM-based Code2Tag, which reads only the solution, is lowest. Every method is under the ten-second budget once loaded, on the one GPU I had: an RTX 2080 Ti with 11 GB. The local methods were timed on it, one problem per call; TF-IDF and the prior run on the CPU, and the API methods run on their providers' servers. Costs: the local methods are free (ModernBERT and F2LLM were timed on an RTX 2080 Ti GPU); the API methods cost about ten cents per thousand problems. The test scores are reported here; the choice itself was made on dev.
