# Jev's words may be searched, as a new method read in sample

The words a TypeSafe Jev method sends ([ADR 0007](0007-api-score-methods-share-the-llm-toolkit.md)) are a convention, a question and the eight tag definitions. A search may rewrite the convention and the tag definitions, the nine pieces of the candidate words; the question (`settings.QUESTION`) stays fixed. The search is GEPA 0.1.4 (`gepa.optimize`, called directly from `methods/typesafe_jev/tuning.py` with an adapter of the project), and its reflection model is `gpt-6-luna` at high reasoning effort, asked through the LLM toolkit's OpenAI adapter with a strict JSON schema. What it finds is a new tagging method, never an edit of the one it starts from: the first, `jev-statement-code-gepa`, starts from the words of `jev-statement-code`, the command line's default ([ADR 0008](0008-cli-default-method.md)), whose words, answers files and frozen files stay as they are.

The search reads the dev set only, in three roles fixed before it runs:

| Folds | Role | Problems |
|---|---|---|
| 1-3 | the learning folds: each iteration draws a minibatch of 40 problems (2 positives of each focus tag, then random others) and shows the worst errors of one piece to the reflection model | 2,382 |
| 4 | the selection fold: every accepted candidate words are scored on all of it | 800 |
| 0 | the held-out fold: no request of the search holds one of its problems, and the adapter refuses their sample ids | 806 |

A problem's score is one minus its balanced Brier term: the mean over the eight focus tags of w_t(y)·(p − y)², with w_t(1) = 1/(2·π_t) and w_t(0) = 1/(2·(1 − π_t)), where π_t is the tag's prevalence in the learning folds, so that a rare tag's positives weigh as much as its negatives. A failed Jev answer scores 0.0 on every tag, as in `answers.probabilities`. Macro F1 with cross-fitted thresholds stays the metric that is reported and compared ([ADR 0001](0001-model-selection-protocol.md)).

**The selection rule is fixed here, before the search.** The chosen candidate words are those with the highest mean score on the selection fold among the candidates that pass the dataset-text check below; a tie goes to the lowest candidate index. Nothing else is chosen after the search: neither a candidate that a fold-0 or test score would favour, nor another rule.

**A search's requests are records of the search, not a method's answers.** Its Jev and reflection answers are cached under the gitignored `experiments/jev-statement-code-gepa/search/`, keyed by request hash, so that running the search again replays it at no cost and then goes on: GEPA's choices come from its seeded generator, and the minibatches from a generator seeded by the iteration. They are never committed. [ADR 0003](0003-api-methods-are-fixed-reference-models.md)'s rule that the answers files are the record applies to the method, not to the search: the searched method answers every dev and test problem again with the chosen words, in its own committed answers files. The selection fold's cached answers are the ones the selection saw, so fresh ones keep Jev's sampling noise out of the frozen dev rows. What `export` commits, once, is the search's record in `assets/methods/<name>/tuning/`: `params.json` (the chosen words and their sha256, the chosen index, the rule, the prevalences, every search setting, GEPA's version, the stop reason and the costs), `candidates.csv` (each candidate's parents, piece, mean score on the selection fold, per-tag balanced Brier score, words' sha256 and check), `candidates/<index>.json` (the words of each candidate that passed the check) and `history.csv` (each iteration's piece, minibatch sums, acceptance or refusal, cost and tokens). A second `export` only checks them.

`export` writes nothing unless three checks pass:

- **The search finished**: it stopped at a cap, not on an error.
- **The seed check**: candidate 0's mean score on the selection fold, from the search's answers, equals the one recomputed from the frozen `scores.csv` of `jev-statement-code`, within `config.TUNING_EXPORT_TOLERANCE`. The seed's cache is filled from that method's committed `answers_dev.jsonl` (folds 1-4), each line checked against the seed request's hash, so the seed costs nothing and the search starts from the frozen method itself.
- **The dataset-text check** (dataset text is never in a method folder, [ADR 0004](0004-tagging-method-framework.md)): no candidate words that `tuning/` would hold may share an 8-word sequence with any problem's statement, notes or solution, after lowercasing and splitting on every character that is not a letter or a digit. Sequences that also occur in the seed words are exempt: those were written by hand, not copied. A candidate that fails keeps its row, hash and reason in `candidates.csv`, without its words, and cannot be chosen. The reflection already refuses a proposal that shares such a sequence with the problems shown in its own prompt.

**Caps bound the cost before it is spent.** The search stops after 200 iterations, or once $15 is spent on Jev and the reflection model together, counted from the caches between iterations (the last iteration overshoots by its own cost, cents). A proposed piece holds at most 1.5 times the seed piece's characters, and a refused proposal (a failed answer, invalid JSON, an empty field, the length cap or a copied sequence) leaves the parent's piece, so that its child cannot be accepted. GEPA's `run_dir` is never set, since its state file is a pickle.

**The searched method is `dev_in_sample`, and a reference.** Its words were chosen while reading the labels of folds 1-4, so it records `method_entries["dev_in_sample"] = true` and is never eligible for the command line (ADR 0004, ADR 0008). Its thresholds are cross-fitted over the five dev folds, as for every Jev method: its dev rows are out of fold for the thresholds, but in sample for the words on folds 1-4. Its honest dev comparison with the method it started from is paired, with group-bootstrap intervals, on the held-out fold, which neither method's words saw; its fold-0 thresholds come from folds 1-4, in sample for its words, which biases that comparison against it, not for it. The run record's `method_entries["search"]` holds the folds, the chosen index, the sha256 of `params.json`, the search's cost and the held-out fold's macro F1 and per-tag F1. Its test part is scored once, after its dev answers are committed, and reported beside the other methods, never used to choose.

**One study per searched method.** ADR 0003 fixes the methods compared in one study before that study's first test request, and ADR 0007's dev-before-test gate covered every method of the package. The gate is now per study (`settings.STUDIES`): the two frozen methods form one study, already answered on test, and the searched method its own, compared with its seed on the held-out fold.

**The frozen methods' words are a prompt file; a searched method's words are data.** `jev-statement` and `jev-statement-code` read `settings.CONVENTION`, `settings.QUESTION` and `settings.TAG_DEFINITIONS` (`SEED_WORDS`), which `settings` reads from the versioned prompt file `templates/jev-tags.v1.yaml` ([ADR 0009](0009-versioned-prompt-files.md)): they were written by hand before the first request, they are in every committed request's hash and in both recipes, and a test rebuilds every committed request from them, byte for byte. A searched method's words are an output: `tuning/params.json` holds them, its method folder copies them to `model/words.json`, its recipe hashes them, and `load` refuses words whose hash differs from `jev.json`'s. Other words are a new search, a new export and a new method name.

**Searched words are a frozen search result, not a prompt version.** [ADR 0009](0009-versioned-prompt-files.md) keeps every prompt version of an API method in one versioned YAML file, mirrored in MLflow's prompt registry: words that a person writes and reviews before their first request. A searched method's words are not written so: a search chose them by a rule fixed before it ran, and `tuning/` records them with the evidence of that choice (every candidate, its score and its check). No `jev-tags.v<N>.yaml` holds them, so the searched method registers no version of `jev-tags` and its run record pins no prompt; its words are pinned by `tuning/params.json`, `model/words.json` and the hashes of `jev.json` and its recipe, and the search's experiment shows each piece as a prompt `jev-gepa-<piece>`, one version per change. The seed is the prompt file's: `SEED_WORDS` is built from `jev-tags.v1.yaml`, so the words are defined once.

## Considered options

- **MLflow's `optimize_prompts`** (MLflow 3.16.1, on GEPA): it takes no validation set, gives every piece the same reflective records, calls the method once per problem with the project's one evaluation worker, reaches its reflection model through litellm (which fetches a price map at import, may drop `reasoning_effort` and reports a cost of 0), always calls `register_prompt`, which safeguard 4 of [ADR 0002](0002-method-folders-and-mlflow-wrapper.md) forbids, and with GEPA 0.1.4 logs only the seed candidate.
- **Nested searches, one per dev fold**: every dev row would be out of sample for its words, and the method could be eligible. It costs five searches (about $50) and gives five sets of words, so a prediction would be five Jev requests, or one set chosen after reading the five searches' scores, which is in sample again.
- **All of dev for the search**: learning and selection would read the five folds, leaving no dev problem that the words did not see; the only out-of-sample comparison with the seed would then be the test set, which ADR 0001 keeps for one report.
- **Macro F1 as GEPA's objective**: GEPA keeps a frontier of per-problem scores and accepts a child on a minibatch's sum, while F1 is a set metric whose thresholds a minibatch of 40 problems cannot fix. The balanced Brier score is a per-problem score of the probabilities themselves, and macro F1 stays the reported metric.
- **GEPA's `run_dir` to resume an interrupted search**: its state file is a pickle. Replaying from the answer caches resumes the search without one.

## Consequences

- The search costs about $10 (about $1.10 of the reflection model and $8.60 of Jev), capped at $15; the searched method's dev and test answers add about $0.67 (4,982 Jev requests), and its timing about $0.01.
- A reader finds in the committed files the words before and after, the chosen candidate and the rule that chose it, that the seed reproduced, every candidate's score and check, and that no committed word copies a problem. The search's own answers stay on the machine that searched: replaying it needs its caches and GEPA 0.1.4, which is pinned and recorded in `params.json`, since its seeded choices are what make a replay give the same candidates.
- The search is logged to MLflow in its own experiment, `jev-gepa`, live while it runs, and replayed from `tuning/` into an empty store by `log-search`, with no paid call; each piece is a prompt with one version per change, made with `create_prompt_version`. It never logs into `tag-methods`, and, as in ADR 0003, MLflow mirrors and gates nothing.
- The held-out fold is small for the rare tags (15 `probabilities` and 16 `games` positives), so its intervals are wide, and "no detectable difference" is a result that notebook 10 reports as such.
- Another search (of `jev-statement`, of other pieces, of the question, or with another reflection model) is another method under another name, with its own `tuning/`, under this protocol.
